Morning routines have a reputation problem. Online, they're often presented as elaborate rituals that start before dawn and take two hours. For most people with jobs, families, or simply imperfect sleep, that's not realistic — and an unrealistic routine is one you'll abandon.

A good morning routine isn't about doing more. It's about starting the day with a little less friction.

## Pick anchors, not a schedule

Instead of a minute-by-minute plan, choose two or three *anchors*: small actions you do every morning regardless of how much time you have. For example:

- drink a glass of water,
- open the curtains or step outside for daylight,
- write down the one thing that matters most today.

Anchors are short enough to survive a late start. On slower mornings, you can add more around them.

## Prepare the night before

The calmest mornings often start the evening before. A five-minute shutdown routine removes decisions you'd otherwise make while groggy:

1. Set out clothes and pack your bag.
2. Prepare anything for breakfast that can be done ahead.
3. Glance at tomorrow's calendar so there are no surprises.
4. Put your phone to charge away from your bed, if you can.

## Protect your first half hour

Checking messages first thing hands your attention to other people's priorities before you've set your own. Try delaying email and social media for the first 20–30 minutes of the day. Use that time for your anchors, breakfast, or simply a quiet coffee.

<Callout type="info" title="Make it easy">
If your phone is your alarm, consider a simple alarm clock instead. It removes the temptation to start scrolling the moment you switch off the alarm.
</Callout>

## Add one thing at a time

If you want your routine to include exercise, reading, or journaling, add one new habit and keep it small at first — five minutes rather than thirty. Once it feels automatic, you can extend it or add something else.

## Plan for imperfect days

Some mornings will go sideways: a bad night's sleep, a sick child, an early meeting. Decide in advance what your "minimum routine" looks like on those days. It might just be water and a moment to write your top priority. Doing the minimum keeps the habit alive without adding guilt.

## A sample 30-minute routine

| Time | Activity |
| --- | --- |
| 0–5 min | Water, open curtains, light stretch |
| 5–15 min | Breakfast without screens |
| 15–20 min | Write today's top priority |
| 20–30 min | Short walk, reading, or quiet time |

Adjust the pieces to fit your life. The goal isn't a perfect morning — it's a reliable one that leaves you feeling slightly more in control. If you like this approach, our guide to [time blocking](/blog/time-blocking-for-real-schedules) helps you carry the same calm into the rest of the day.
