Many trips are planned like a checklist: as many cities, landmarks, and photos as possible in the time available. Slow travel takes the opposite approach. You stay longer in fewer places, give yourself room to wander, and experience a destination more like a temporary resident than a visitor passing through.

## Why travel slowly?

- **Less time in transit.** Every move between cities costs hours of packing, checking out, and getting from one place to the next.
- **Lower costs.** Longer stays often unlock weekly rates for accommodation, and shopping at local markets is cheaper than eating out for every meal.
- **Deeper experiences.** You notice the rhythm of a neighborhood, return to a favorite café, and have time for unplanned discoveries.
- **A lighter footprint.** Fewer long-distance journeys generally means less travel-related pollution.

## Choose fewer places

As a rough guide, consider at least four or five nights in each base, and a week or more if you can. For a two-week trip, two bases is often plenty. Use day trips to see nearby towns without changing accommodation.

<Callout type="tip" title="A useful test">
If you'd spend more time traveling to a place than you'd spend in it, consider leaving it for another trip.
</Callout>

## Pick a good base

A good base makes slow travel easy:

- **Kitchen access** lets you shop at local markets and cook some meals.
- **Walkable surroundings** encourage exploring on foot.
- **Transit links** make day trips simple.
- **A comfortable workspace** matters if you'll work remotely part of the time.

Read reviews with an eye for long-stay comments — noise, Wi-Fi reliability, and how well the place works for daily life rather than a single night.

## Plan loosely

Slow travel doesn't mean no planning. It means planning *anchors* rather than every hour:

1. List a few experiences you'd be disappointed to miss.
2. Book anything that requires advance reservations.
3. Leave most days open, with a short list of optional ideas.

Unplanned time is where many of the best travel memories come from.

## Connect with the place

- Learn a handful of phrases in the local language: greetings, please, thank you.
- Visit the same café or market more than once.
- Join a local class, tour run by residents, or community event.
- Learn about local customs before you arrive and respect them.

## A sample two-week outline

| Days | Plan |
| --- | --- |
| 1–7 | City base: neighborhoods, museums, markets, two day trips |
| 8 | Travel day by train |
| 9–14 | Countryside or coastal base: walks, local food, rest |

## Bringing slow travel home
