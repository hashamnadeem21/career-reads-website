Most weeks end with a vague sense of unfinished business: notes on scraps of paper, emails flagged "for later," projects that haven't moved. A weekly review is a short, regular appointment with yourself to gather those loose ends and decide what happens next.

Thirty minutes once a week can save hours of scrambling — and reduce the background stress of wondering what you've forgotten.

## When to do it

Pick a consistent time. Friday afternoon works well for many people because the week is fresh in mind. Others prefer Sunday evening or Monday morning. Consistency matters more than the specific slot, so put it on your calendar as a recurring event.

## The five-part template

### 1. Collect (5 minutes)

Gather everything that needs attention into one place: sticky notes, notebook pages, flagged emails, voice memos, browser tabs you've been saving. Don't decide anything yet — just collect.

### 2. Clear (10 minutes)

Work through what you collected. For each item, decide:

- **Do it now** if it takes less than two minutes.
- **Schedule it** if it needs a specific time.
- **Add it to a project list** if it's part of something bigger.
- **Delete it** if it no longer matters.

### 3. Check projects (5 minutes)

Look at each active project and ask: *what is the next concrete action?* A project without a next action tends to stall without anyone noticing.

<Callout type="tip" title="Keep a single list">
Keep active projects on one list. When the list gets long, that's useful information — it may be time to pause something.
</Callout>

### 4. Look back (5 minutes)

Briefly reflect on the week:

- What went well?
- What took longer than expected?
- What would you do differently?

Writing two or three sentences is enough. Over time, these notes reveal patterns.

### 5. Plan ahead (5 minutes)

Open next week's calendar. Note fixed commitments, then choose your top three priorities for the week. If you use [time blocking](/blog/time-blocking-for-real-schedules), place focus blocks for those priorities now.

## A simple weekly review checklist

| Step | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Collect | Is everything that needs attention in one place? |
| Clear | Has every item been done, scheduled, filed, or deleted? |
| Projects | Does each active project have a next action? |
| Look back | What did I learn this week? |
| Plan | What are my top three priorities for next week? |

## Making the habit stick

- **Protect the time.** Treat the review like a meeting you can't skip.
- **Make it pleasant.** A favorite drink or a quiet spot helps.
- **Keep it short.** If you skip a week, do a shorter version next time rather than abandoning the habit.

The weekly review isn't glamorous, but it's one of the most reliable ways to stay on top of work without carrying everything in your head.
