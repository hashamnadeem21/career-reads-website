Traveling with only a carry-on can feel impossible until you try it. Then it's hard to go back: no waiting at the baggage carousel, no lost luggage, and far less to haul up stairs and onto trains. Two weeks fits in a carry-on more easily than most people expect — the trick is planning around laundry rather than packing for every possible day.

## Choose the right bag

First, check the size and weight limits for every airline you'll fly, including smaller regional carriers, which can be stricter. Then consider:

- **Backpack or wheeled case?** Backpacks are better for cobblestones, stairs, and trains. Wheeled cases are easier in airports and on smooth pavements.
- **Weight of the bag itself.** A lighter empty bag leaves more room in the weight allowance.
- **Simple layout.** One large compartment is often easier to pack than many small pockets.

## Pack for one week, not two

Plan to do laundry once mid-trip, either at your accommodation, a laundromat, or by hand in a sink. This halves what you need to bring.

<Callout type="tip" title="The laundry plan">
Look up laundry options at your accommodation before you go. Even a small bottle of travel detergent makes sink-washing quick.
</Callout>

## Build a mix-and-match wardrobe

Choose clothes in a limited color palette so nearly every top works with every bottom. A sample two-week list for mild weather:

| Item | Quantity |
| --- | --- |
| Tops | 5–6 |
| Bottoms | 2–3 |
| Light layer (sweater or fleece) | 1 |
| Rain-resistant jacket | 1 (worn while traveling) |
| Underwear and socks | 6–7 each |
| Sleepwear | 1 set |
| Shoes | 2 pairs (wear the bulkiest) |

Wear your heaviest items — jacket, boots, sweater — on travel days to save space.

## Pack toiletries smartly

Liquid rules vary by airport, so check them before you fly. Ways to save space:

- Choose solid toiletries such as bar shampoo and solid deodorant where you can.
- Decant products into small reusable containers.
- Buy bulky items like sunscreen at your destination.

## Use packing cubes or a folding system

Packing cubes keep categories separate and make it easy to find things without unpacking everything. Rolling soft clothes and folding structured items flat both work — use whichever keeps things tidiest for you.

## Don't forget the essentials

Keep these in a small bag that stays with you:

- passport or ID, and copies stored separately,
- phone, charger, and a universal adapter if needed,
- any medication in its original packaging,
- a reusable water bottle (empty through security).

## Do a test pack

A few days before the trip, pack everything and carry the bag around your home or up some stairs. If it feels too heavy or doesn't close easily, remove a few items. You'll rarely miss them.

Packing light is a skill that improves with each trip. Start with one carry-on-only trip, notice what you didn't use, and pack even lighter next time. If you're planning a longer journey, our guide to [slow travel](/blog/slow-travel-planning-guide) pairs well with packing light.
