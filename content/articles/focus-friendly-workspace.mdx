Working from home offers flexibility, but it also blends work into the same spaces where you relax, eat, and spend time with family. A thoughtfully set up workspace — even a small corner — helps your brain switch into focus mode.

## Define a dedicated spot

If you can, work in the same place each day. Over time, sitting down there becomes a cue to focus. It doesn't need to be a separate room. A consistent corner of a table works if you set it up and pack it away the same way each day.

## Get the light right

Natural light can make a space feel better to work in. Position your desk near a window if possible, ideally to the side so you don't face glare on your screen or have the window directly behind you on video calls. In the evening, a desk lamp with warm, even light is easier on the eyes than a single harsh overhead light.

## Set up for comfort

Discomfort pulls attention away from work. A few basics:

- **Screen at roughly eye level.** A laptop stand or a stack of books can help.
- **Elbows near 90 degrees** when typing, with shoulders relaxed.
- **Feet flat** on the floor or a footrest.
- **Move regularly.** Stand, stretch, or walk briefly every hour.

<Callout type="info" title="Small upgrade, big difference">
If you use a laptop all day, an external keyboard and mouse combined with a stand is one of the most effective low-cost upgrades.
</Callout>

## Manage noise

Noise is one of the biggest focus killers at home. Options include:

- noise-cancelling headphones,
- a fan or white-noise app to mask intermittent sounds,
- agreeing on "focus hours" with others in your home,
- soft furnishings like rugs and curtains to reduce echo.

## Reduce visual clutter

Keep only what you need for today's work on the desk. A small tray or drawer for everything else prevents piles from forming. Visual clutter competes for attention in subtle ways.

## Set up your devices for focus

Your digital workspace matters as much as your physical one:

1. **Turn on a focus mode** during deep work to silence non-urgent notifications.
2. **Close unneeded tabs and apps** before starting a task.
3. **Keep your phone out of reach** — another room is better than face-down on the desk.
4. **Use separate browser profiles** for work and personal browsing.

## Create start and stop rituals

Without a commute, the workday can bleed into the evening. Simple rituals help:

| Ritual | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Start | Make a drink, review your top three tasks, put on headphones |
| Midday | Step outside for a short walk |
| Stop | Write tomorrow's first task, close the laptop, tidy the desk |

## Start with one change

You don't need to overhaul everything. Pick the change that addresses your biggest distraction — noise, posture, or notifications — and start there. Then combine your new setup with [time blocking](/blog/time-blocking-for-real-schedules) to make the most of your focused hours.
