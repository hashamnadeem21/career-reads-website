Physical clutter is easy to see. Digital clutter hides in notification badges, a bursting inbox, a downloads folder nobody has opened in months, and thousands of near-duplicate photos. It doesn't take up space in your home, but it does take up attention.

This plan breaks the job into manageable sessions across one weekend.

## Saturday morning: your phone

Your phone is the device you touch most, so start there.

### Tame notifications

Go to your notification settings and ask one question for each app: *does this need to interrupt me?* Messages from people usually do. Promotions, news alerts, and most app reminders usually don't. Turn off anything that isn't time-sensitive.

### Clear the home screen

Keep only the apps you use daily on the first screen. Move everything else into a folder or the app library. Fewer icons means fewer impulsive taps.

### Delete unused apps

If you haven't opened an app in three months, delete it. You can almost always reinstall it later.

## Saturday afternoon: your inbox

An overflowing inbox makes it hard to see what matters.

1. **Unsubscribe in bulk.** Search for "unsubscribe" and work through newsletters and promotions you no longer read.
2. **Archive the backlog.** Select everything older than a month and archive it. It's still searchable; it's just out of sight.
3. **Create a few filters.** Route receipts, newsletters, and notifications into labels or folders automatically.

<Callout type="tip" title="Don't aim for perfection">
"Inbox zero" isn't required. The goal is an inbox where important messages are easy to spot.
</Callout>

## Sunday morning: files and downloads

### Empty your downloads folder

Move anything worth keeping into a proper folder and delete the rest. Installers, duplicate PDFs, and old screenshots are the usual culprits.

### Use a simple folder structure

A shallow structure is easier to maintain than a deep one. For example:

| Folder | What goes in it |
| --- | --- |
| Admin | Bills, contracts, insurance, taxes |
| Projects | Active work, one folder per project |
| Archive | Finished projects and old documents |
| Personal | Hobbies, travel plans, personal writing |

## Sunday afternoon: photos and backups

Photo libraries grow quietly. Start by deleting obvious duplicates, blurry shots, and screenshots you no longer need. Most photo apps can surface these for you.

Then check your backups. Make sure important files and photos exist in at least two places — for example, on your device and in a cloud backup. A declutter is a good moment to confirm that backups are actually running.

## Keep it tidy with small rules

Decluttering once helps; simple rules keep it from creeping back:

- Unsubscribe the moment an unwanted newsletter arrives.
- Clear your downloads folder every Friday.
- Review notification settings whenever you install a new app.
- Delete screenshots once you've used them.

A tidier digital space won't change your life overnight, but many people find they reach for their phone a little less — and feel a little calmer when they do. Pair this with a [calm morning routine](/blog/calm-morning-routine) for a gentler start to each day.
